Selling a business is more than agreeing on a price and signing a sale agreement.

For business owners, the legal side of a transaction can affect what is being sold, how the deal is structured, what liabilities remain with the seller, and how much protection the seller has after completion.

Preparing these matters early can make the sale process smoother and help prevent avoidable surprises during buyer due diligence.

What Legal Matters Should a Business Owner Consider Before a Sale?

The key areas generally include:

  • Ownership and corporate structure
  • Sale structure
  • Commercial contracts
  • Intellectual property
  • Employees and management
  • Licences and regulatory compliance
  • Litigation and liabilities
  • Confidentiality
  • Transaction documentation
  • Post-sale obligations

The exact requirements will depend on the nature and structure of the business.

1. Confirm Ownership of the Business

Before selling, the seller should ensure that ownership is clear and properly documented.

This may involve reviewing:

  • Share ownership
  • Shareholder agreements
  • Previous transfers
  • Corporate records
  • Restrictions on transferring shares

If there are multiple shareholders, their rights and consent requirements should also be considered before entering negotiations.

A buyer needs confidence that the person selling the business has the legal authority to do so.

2. Decide How the Business Will Be Sold

One of the first legal considerations is the transaction structure.

Depending on the circumstances, a business may be sold through:

Share Sale

The buyer acquires the shares of the company and, generally, the company continues with its existing assets, contracts and obligations.

Asset or Business Sale

The buyer acquires specified assets or business operations rather than the shares of the company.

The appropriate structure depends on the business, liabilities, regulatory requirements and commercial objectives of both parties.

3. Review Your Commercial Contracts

A buyer will likely examine the company's important contracts.

Before a sale, review agreements with:

  • Customers
  • Suppliers
  • Distributors
  • Partners
  • Service providers

Particular attention should be given to change-of-control, assignment and termination provisions.

A major contract that cannot be transferred—or can be terminated following a change in ownership—may affect the transaction.

4. Check Intellectual Property Ownership

The seller should be able to demonstrate that the business owns or has the necessary rights to use its key intellectual property.

This may include:

  • Trademarks
  • Software
  • Technology
  • Website content
  • Brand assets
  • Databases
  • Proprietary processes

Where IP was created by employees, consultants or third parties, appropriate ownership or licensing arrangements should be documented.

5. Review Employees and Key Management

Employees can be an important part of the value and continuity of a business.

Consider:

  • Employment agreements
  • Key employee arrangements
  • Outstanding employee claims
  • Benefits and obligations
  • Confidentiality provisions
  • Post-employment restrictions

The transaction may also require consideration of how employees will be treated following completion.

6. Review Licences and Regulatory Compliance

A business should be able to demonstrate that it is properly licensed and compliant with the requirements applicable to its activities.

Depending on the industry, this may include:

  • Trade licences
  • Regulatory approvals
  • Sector-specific permits
  • Registrations
  • Compliance obligations

Any material compliance issue should ideally be identified before entering detailed negotiations.

7. Identify Litigation and Potential Liabilities

Existing or potential legal claims can become an important part of buyer due diligence.

The seller should understand its position regarding:

  • Current litigation
  • Threatened claims
  • Contractual disputes
  • Regulatory matters
  • Guarantees
  • Indemnities
  • Other material obligations

The question is not necessarily whether every issue must be resolved before the sale.

It is whether the parties understand the risk and how it will be allocated in the transaction.

8. Protect Confidential Information

Selling a business requires sharing sensitive information with potential buyers.

Before providing commercially sensitive information, appropriate confidentiality arrangements should be considered.

These may cover:

  • Financial information
  • Customer information
  • Pricing
  • Contracts
  • Intellectual property
  • Business strategies
  • Employee information

Confidentiality becomes particularly important where discussions take place with multiple potential buyers.

9. Understand the Sale Agreement

The sale agreement is one of the most important documents in the transaction.

Depending on the structure, it may address:

  • Purchase price
  • Payment terms
  • Conditions to completion
  • Representations and warranties
  • Indemnities
  • Liability limitations
  • Completion obligations
  • Confidentiality
  • Non-compete or restrictive covenants
  • Post-completion obligations

The seller should understand not only what they are receiving, but also what legal obligations they are accepting.

10. Consider Your Obligations After the Sale

The transaction may not completely end the seller's involvement.

Depending on the agreement, the seller may be required to:

  • Assist with the transition
  • Remain involved for a defined period
  • Provide information after completion
  • Comply with restrictive covenants
  • Honour certain warranties or indemnities

These obligations should be understood before signing.

Why Should Sellers Conduct Their Own Legal Review?

Many sellers wait for the buyer's legal team to identify issues.

A better approach is to conduct a seller-side legal readiness review before entering the market.

This can help identify:

  • Missing documents
  • Contractual restrictions
  • Ownership issues
  • Compliance gaps
  • Potential liabilities
  • Weak contractual protections

The advantage is simple:

It is better to discover and address a legal issue before the buyer does.

Legal Preparation Can Strengthen Your Negotiating Position

Legal preparation is not simply about avoiding problems.

It can help sellers enter negotiations with a clearer understanding of:

What they own → What they are selling → What risks exist → What protections they need → What obligations they are accepting

That clarity can make a significant difference when negotiating the final transaction terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main legal considerations when selling a business?

The main considerations include ownership, transaction structure, contracts, intellectual property, employees, licences, liabilities, litigation, confidentiality and the terms of the sale agreement.

Should I conduct legal due diligence before selling my business?

Yes. A seller-side legal review can identify issues before the buyer conducts its own due diligence, giving the seller an opportunity to address or properly manage them.

What is the difference between a share sale and an asset sale?

In a share sale, the buyer generally acquires ownership of the company. In an asset sale, the buyer acquires specified assets or business operations. The appropriate structure depends on the transaction.

Can contracts affect the sale of a business?

Yes. Change-of-control, assignment and termination provisions may affect whether important contracts continue following the sale.

What happens to liabilities when a business is sold?

The treatment of liabilities depends heavily on the transaction structure and the terms agreed between the parties. This should be assessed carefully before signing.

What legal documents are needed to sell a business?

Depending on the transaction, documents may include a confidentiality agreement, letter of intent or heads of terms, sale and purchase agreement, disclosure documents, shareholder documents and ancillary agreements.
