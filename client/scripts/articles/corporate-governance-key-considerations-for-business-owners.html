Corporate governance is often associated with large corporations, boards and formal policies. In reality, good governance matters at every stage of a business.

As a company grows, informal decision-making can become difficult to manage. Ownership may become more complex, responsibilities may overlap, and important decisions may be made without clear authority or documentation.

Effective corporate governance provides a framework for how a business is directed, controlled and held accountable.

For business owners, the objective is not to create unnecessary bureaucracy. It is to establish enough structure to protect the business, manage risk and support better decisions.

What Is Corporate Governance?

Corporate governance refers to the systems, processes and responsibilities used to manage and oversee a company.

It helps answer fundamental questions such as:

  • Who has authority to make decisions?
  • Who is responsible for specific matters?
  • How are major decisions approved?
  • How are conflicts of interest managed?
  • How are company records maintained?
  • How are shareholders and management held accountable?

The appropriate governance framework will depend on the size, ownership structure, activities and jurisdiction of the business.

1. Clearly Define Roles and Responsibilities

One of the most common governance weaknesses is unclear responsibility.

Business owners should establish clear roles for:

  • Shareholders
  • Directors
  • Managers
  • Executives
  • Key employees

Everyone involved should understand what they can decide, what requires approval and where accountability sits.

2. Establish Clear Decision-Making Authority

Not every business decision should require shareholder or board approval.

A governance framework should distinguish between:

Day-to-day decisions Handled by management within agreed authority.

Material decisions May require board or shareholder approval depending on the company's structure and governing documents.

These may include major investments, borrowing, acquisitions, disposals or significant contracts.

Clear authority can reduce delays and prevent unauthorised commitments.

3. Maintain Proper Corporate Records

Corporate decisions should be properly documented.

This may include:

  • Board resolutions
  • Shareholder resolutions
  • Meeting minutes
  • Corporate registers
  • Material agreements
  • Regulatory filings
  • Ownership records

Good records demonstrate that important decisions were properly considered and authorised.

4. Review Shareholder Arrangements

As businesses grow, shareholder relationships can become more complex.

Where appropriate, shareholders should consider arrangements covering:

  • Ownership
  • Voting rights
  • Decision-making
  • Transfer of shares
  • Restrictions on transfers
  • Dividends
  • Deadlock
  • Exit arrangements
  • Dispute resolution

A well-structured shareholder agreement can help prevent disagreements from becoming major business problems.

5. Manage Conflicts of Interest

Conflicts can arise when a director, shareholder or manager has a personal interest in a company transaction or decision.

Businesses should have a clear approach for:

  • Identifying conflicts
  • Disclosing conflicts
  • Recording them
  • Managing affected decisions

The objective is to ensure that decisions are made in the company's best interests and with appropriate transparency.

6. Strengthen Financial Oversight

Good governance also requires appropriate financial oversight.

Business owners should establish processes for:

  • Financial reporting
  • Budgeting
  • Cash management
  • Expense approvals
  • Financial controls
  • Management reporting

Owners and directors should have sufficient financial information to understand how the business is performing and where risks may be emerging.

7. Establish Appropriate Internal Controls

Internal controls help protect the company against errors, misuse of funds and operational risks.

Depending on the business, controls may cover:

  • Payments
  • Bank access
  • Procurement
  • Expenses
  • Payroll
  • Accounting
  • Inventory
  • Authorisation limits

Controls should be proportionate to the size and complexity of the business.

8. Review Legal and Regulatory Compliance

Governance should include mechanisms for monitoring applicable legal and regulatory obligations.

Depending on the business, this may involve:

  • Corporate filings
  • Licensing
  • Employment requirements
  • Tax obligations
  • Data protection
  • Industry regulations
  • Contractual obligations

Compliance should be treated as an ongoing responsibility rather than an annual exercise.

9. Protect Company Information and Assets

Businesses should establish appropriate controls over:

  • Confidential information
  • Intellectual property
  • Customer data
  • Financial information
  • Digital systems
  • Physical assets

Access should be granted based on legitimate business requirements, with appropriate controls around sensitive information.

10. Plan for Business Continuity and Succession

Governance should not depend entirely on one individual.

Business owners should consider:

  • Management succession
  • Delegation of authority
  • Key-person dependency
  • Emergency decision-making
  • Business continuity
  • Ownership transition

This becomes particularly important when preparing for investment, expansion or a future business sale.

Why Corporate Governance Matters as a Business Grows

Informal management may work when a business has a small number of people.

As the business grows, however, informal arrangements can create:

  • Unclear authority
  • Shareholder disputes
  • Unauthorised decisions
  • Financial control weaknesses
  • Compliance risks
  • Management conflicts
  • Difficulties during due diligence

Good governance provides the structure needed to grow without losing control.

Corporate Governance Is Not About Creating More Bureaucracy

For many business owners, the concern is that governance will create unnecessary procedures and slow down decision-making.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is corporate governance?

Corporate governance is the framework of rules, responsibilities, processes and controls used to direct and oversee a company.

Why is corporate governance important for small businesses?

As a business grows, clear governance can help prevent unclear authority, shareholder disputes, financial control weaknesses and compliance risks.

What are the key elements of corporate governance?

Key elements include clear roles and authority, shareholder arrangements, corporate records, financial oversight, internal controls, conflict management and regulatory compliance.

Does every business need a board of directors?

The requirements depend on the company's legal structure, jurisdiction and applicable regulations. However, every business can benefit from clear decision-making and accountability, even where a formal board structure is not required.

What is the relationship between corporate governance and business growth?

Good governance can provide the structure needed to scale operations, manage risk, attract investors and support future transactions without losing control of the business.

Can poor corporate governance affect business value?

Yes. Weak governance, unclear ownership, poor records or unresolved shareholder issues can create additional risks for investors and buyers and may affect the attractiveness of a business.
