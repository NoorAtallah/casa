Business owners make strategic decisions every day. But as a business grows, those decisions become more complex—and the consequences of getting them wrong become greater.

Sometimes the need for strategic advice is obvious: declining profitability, cash-flow pressure, expansion challenges or a major investment.

Other times, the business may appear successful while important strategic questions remain unanswered.

Strategic advisory support can help business owners step back from day-to-day operations, understand the bigger picture and make better-informed decisions about the future.

The question is not whether every business needs an advisor.

It is when external strategic advice can create meaningful value.

What Is Strategic Advisory Support?

Strategic advisory support helps business owners and management evaluate where the business is today, where it wants to go and what needs to happen to get there.

It can involve:

  • Business strategy
  • Growth planning
  • Financial analysis
  • Market and opportunity assessment
  • Investment decisions
  • Risk assessment
  • Business restructuring
  • Expansion planning
  • Performance improvement
  • Strategic decision-making

The role of an advisor is not to run the business for the owner.

It is to provide independent insight, challenge assumptions and help management make important decisions with greater clarity.

1. You Are Making Decisions Based Mainly on Instinct

Experience and intuition are valuable business assets.

However, major decisions should ideally be supported by reliable information.

If you are considering an investment, acquisition, expansion or restructuring but cannot clearly answer:

  • What will it cost?
  • What return should we expect?
  • What are the major risks?
  • What happens if assumptions change?
  • What alternatives do we have?

then strategic advisory support may be valuable.

2. The Business Is Growing but Becoming Harder to Manage

Growth can create a new set of problems.

As revenue and operations increase, businesses often face:

  • More complex finances
  • Higher working-capital requirements
  • Larger teams
  • More contracts
  • Greater operational complexity
  • Increased regulatory exposure
  • More demanding customers
  • Greater management pressure

Growth is positive—but uncontrolled complexity can reduce profitability and increase risk.

An advisor can help management identify where the business needs to strengthen before the next stage of growth.

3. Revenue Is Growing but Profit Is Not

This is an important warning sign.

If revenue is increasing but profitability is not improving, management should investigate why.

Possible causes include:

  • Pricing problems
  • Increasing costs
  • Low-margin customers
  • Inefficient operations
  • Excessive overhead
  • Poor product or service mix
  • Weak cost controls

Strategic advisory can help move the conversation from:

“How do we generate more revenue?”

to:

“How do we create more profitable and sustainable revenue?”

4. You Are Experiencing Cash-Flow Pressure

Profit and cash are not the same thing.

A business can report a profit while struggling to meet its short-term obligations.

Warning signs include:

  • Increasing receivables
  • Delayed customer payments
  • Supplier pressure
  • Reliance on short-term financing
  • Difficulty funding growth
  • Limited visibility over future cash requirements

Strategic and financial advisory can help identify the underlying causes and develop a more structured approach to cash flow and working capital.

5. You Are Considering Expansion

Expansion is one of the most common situations where strategic advice can add value.

Before entering a new market, location, product line or business activity, consider:

  • Market demand
  • Capital requirements
  • Expected return
  • Competition
  • Management capacity
  • Operational readiness
  • Legal and regulatory requirements
  • Downside scenarios

The key question is:

Is the business ready to expand—or simply eager to expand?

6. You Are Considering a Major Investment

A major investment deserves more than an attractive business case.

Management should evaluate:

  • Financial return
  • Cash-flow impact
  • Funding requirements
  • Strategic fit
  • Implementation risk
  • Opportunity cost
  • Exit or downside scenarios

An independent advisor can challenge the assumptions behind the investment and help determine whether the opportunity is commercially justified and financially sustainable.

7. The Business Has Become Too Dependent on the Owner

This is common in founder-led businesses.

If the owner is responsible for most:

  • Major decisions
  • Customer relationships
  • Sales
  • Financial approvals
  • Supplier relationships
  • Operational problem-solving

then the business may struggle to scale without the owner's constant involvement.

Strategic advisory can help identify opportunities to improve:

  • Delegation
  • Governance
  • Management structure
  • Processes
  • Reporting
  • Accountability

This can make the business more scalable—and potentially more valuable.

8. You Are Unsure Which Opportunity to Pursue

Business owners frequently face competing opportunities.

For example:

  • Expand into a new market
  • Invest in the existing business
  • Acquire a competitor
  • Launch a new service
  • Hire senior management
  • Reduce debt

The challenge is not finding opportunities.

It is determining which opportunity deserves the company's capital, time and attention.

Strategic advisory can provide a structured framework for comparing alternatives based on return, risk, resources and strategic fit.

9. You Are Preparing for Investment

Investors look beyond revenue.

They may assess:

  • Financial performance
  • Business model
  • Growth potential
  • Management
  • Risks
  • Governance
  • Contracts
  • Financial controls
  • Market position
  • Future projections

Strategic advisory can help businesses identify weaknesses and improve their investment readiness before approaching investors.

10. You Are Considering Selling the Business

A business sale should ideally be prepared well before the business goes to market.

Strategic preparation may involve:

  • Improving profitability
  • Strengthening financial reporting
  • Addressing legal risks
  • Reviewing contracts
  • Reducing operational dependencies
  • Improving governance
  • Preparing management information
  • Identifying potential value drivers

The objective is not simply to sell the business.

It is to make the business more attractive, more defensible and better prepared for due diligence.

11. You Keep Solving the Same Problems

Recurring problems are often a sign that the underlying issue has not been addressed.

For example:

Cash-flow problems → repeated collections issues Staff problems → unclear responsibilities Profitability problems → weak pricing or cost structure Operational problems → inefficient processes Customer problems → poor service model

If management repeatedly treats the symptom without addressing the cause, external strategic support can provide an objective perspective.

12. You Need an Independent Challenge

Sometimes business owners already have a preferred answer.

The value of an external advisor may be in asking:

“What if your assumption is wrong?”

An independent advisor can challenge:

  • Financial assumptions
  • Growth expectations
  • Market assumptions
  • Investment returns
  • Risk assessments
  • Strategic priorities

This does not mean challenging the owner for the sake of it.

It means testing the decision before the market does.

13. The Business Is Entering a New Stage

Different stages of a business require different priorities.

Early Stage

Focus may be on:

  • Business model
  • Funding
  • Market validation
  • Cash preservation

Growth Stage

Focus may shift toward:

  • Scalability
  • Profitability
  • Systems
  • Management
  • Working capital

Mature Stage

Priorities may include:

  • Optimisation
  • Expansion
  • Investment
  • Governance
  • Succession
  • Business value

Strategic advisory can help businesses adapt their approach as their needs change.

Strategic Advisory Is Not Only for Businesses in Trouble

One of the most important misconceptions is that businesses only need advisors when something is wrong.

In reality, the strongest time to seek strategic advice can be before a major decision is made.

A business that is profitable, growing and financially healthy may be in the best position to:

  • Expand
  • Invest
  • Acquire
  • Raise capital
  • Enter new markets
  • Improve its structure
  • Prepare for a future sale

The purpose of strategic advisory is therefore not simply to fix problems.

It is to help businesses make better decisions before problems arise.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is strategic advisory support?

Strategic advisory support helps business owners evaluate major business decisions, opportunities and risks and develop a clear strategy for moving forward.

When does a business need a strategic advisor?

A business may benefit from a strategic advisor when it is growing rapidly, considering expansion or investment, experiencing profitability or cash-flow challenges, preparing for investors or a sale, or facing a complex strategic decision.

Is strategic advisory only for large companies?

No. Strategic advisory can be particularly valuable for SMEs and founder-led businesses, where owners often manage multiple strategic, financial and operational responsibilities themselves.

What does a strategic business advisor do?

A strategic advisor provides independent analysis, challenges assumptions, evaluates options, identifies risks and helps management develop practical strategies.

Why should a business use an external strategic advisor?

An external advisor can provide objective expertise and an independent perspective that may be difficult to obtain from people who are closely involved in the business.

When should a business seek strategic advice?

Ideally, before a major decision is made—particularly when the decision involves significant capital, expansion, investment, acquisition, restructuring or material business risk.

Can strategic advisory improve business profitability?

It can help identify the underlying drivers of poor profitability, including pricing, costs, customer mix, operational inefficiencies and resource allocation, and develop strategies to address them.

Does a strategic advisor make decisions for the business owner?

No. The advisor provides analysis, recommendations and options. The business owner remains responsible for the final decision.
