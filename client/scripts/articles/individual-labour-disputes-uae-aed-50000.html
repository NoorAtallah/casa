The resolution of individual labour disputes in the United Arab Emirates has undergone a significant procedural development in recent years. One of the most important changes was introduced by Federal Decree-Law No. (20) of 2023, which amended Article 54 of Federal Decree-Law No. (33) of 2021 Regarding the Regulation of Labour Relations (the “UAE Labour Law”).

Under the amended Article 54, the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) is empowered, in specified circumstances, not merely to facilitate an amicable settlement between an employee and an employer, but to adjudicate the dispute and issue a decision having the force of an enforceable instrument.

This mechanism is particularly important where the value of the claim does not exceed AED 50,000. It represents a departure from the traditional model under which unresolved labour disputes would ultimately be referred to the competent court.

The amended framework therefore creates a specialized administrative dispute-resolution mechanism designed to provide faster and more efficient determination of certain individual employment disputes.

The Legal Framework: Article 54 of the UAE Labour Law

Article 54 of Federal Decree-Law No. (33) of 2021 regulates individual labour disputes arising between employers, workers, or their respective beneficiaries.

Under Article 54, a party asserting a right arising from the employment relationship must initially submit the dispute to MOHRE. The Ministry examines the complaint and takes the necessary steps to seek an amicable settlement.

The amended legislation, however, goes further by granting MOHRE adjudicatory powers in specific circumstances.

In particular, MOHRE may determine the dispute where:

The value of the claim does not exceed AED 50,000; or

The dispute concerns a party’s failure to comply with a previous amicable settlement decision issued by MOHRE, regardless of the value of the claim.

This amendment was introduced by Federal Decree-Law No. (20) of 2023, which expressly replaced Article 54 with the amended framework.

Is the AED 50,000 MOHRE Adjudication System a New Mechanism?

Yes.

One of the most significant legal developments is that the authority of MOHRE to adjudicate individual labour disputes involving claims of up to AED 50,000 was introduced through the 2023 amendment.

The original framework of Federal Decree-Law No. (33) of 2021 primarily contemplated MOHRE’s role in examining labour complaints and attempting amicable settlement, with unresolved disputes being referred to the competent court.

The 2023 amendment fundamentally expanded this role by allowing MOHRE to issue binding decisions in qualifying disputes.

Accordingly, the amended Article 54 should be understood as creating an administrative adjudication mechanism within the UAE labour dispute-resolution system, rather than merely another form of mediation.

1. MOHRE’s Adjudicatory Jurisdiction

The first major legal issue under Article 54 is the scope of MOHRE’s authority.

The Ministry’s adjudicatory jurisdiction is not unlimited. It applies principally to two categories of disputes.

A. Claims Not Exceeding AED 50,000

The first category concerns individual labour disputes where the value of the claim does not exceed AED 50,000.

For example, a worker may claim:

  • unpaid wages
  • end-of-service benefits
  • payment in lieu of annual leave
  • notice-period compensation
  • overtime payments
  • other employment-related contractual or statutory entitlements

Where the total claim falls within the statutory threshold, MOHRE may adjudicate the dispute rather than merely attempt to facilitate settlement.

B. Non-Compliance with a Previous MOHRE Settlement Decision

The second category is broader.

MOHRE may also adjudicate a dispute where either party fails to comply with a previous amicable settlement decision issued by MOHRE, regardless of the amount of the claim.

This means that the AED 50,000 threshold does not apply to this second category.

Consequently, the jurisdictional basis is not exclusively determined by the monetary value of the dispute. The existence of a prior MOHRE settlement decision and a subsequent failure to comply with it may independently trigger MOHRE’s adjudicatory authority.

2. The Legal Effect of a MOHRE Decision

One of the most important features of the amended Article 54 is the legal effect of the Ministry’s decision.

The provision expressly states that a MOHRE decision issued within its statutory adjudicatory jurisdiction has the force of an executive instrument and may be endorsed with the executory formula in accordance with the applicable procedures.

This is particularly significant because it gives the administrative decision an enforcement effect comparable, in practical terms, to an enforceable judicial instrument.

Accordingly, the mechanism may be summarized as follows:

Labour complaint → MOHRE examination → amicable settlement attempt → MOHRE adjudication where Article 54 applies → enforceable decision.

The mechanism therefore enables certain labour disputes to be determined without requiring the parties to commence ordinary litigation before the courts in the first instance.

3. Can a MOHRE Decision Be Challenged Before the Courts?

Yes.

The fact that MOHRE may issue an enforceable decision does not eliminate judicial review.

Under Article 54, either party may bring proceedings before the competent court challenging the Ministry’s decision within 15 working days from the date on which the party is notified of, or served with, the decision.

This deadline is particularly important because failure to comply with the statutory period may have serious procedural consequences.

Therefore, an employer or employee who disagrees with the MOHRE decision should carefully calculate the statutory deadline from the date of notification or service.

4. What Happens When a Court Action Is Filed?

The amended Article 54 establishes an accelerated judicial procedure.

Once the action is filed, the competent court is required to schedule a hearing within three working days from registration of the case, with the parties being duly notified.

The court is then required to determine the dispute within the statutory period prescribed by Article 54.

Importantly, the filing of the court action results in the suspension of enforcement of the MOHRE decision.

This creates a balance between administrative efficiency and judicial protection.

On the one hand, MOHRE’s decision is given enforceable status. On the other hand, the party challenging the decision is protected from enforcement while the court reviews the dispute.

5. The Judgment of the Competent Court

Another distinctive feature of the amended mechanism is the finality of the judgment issued by the competent court in proceedings challenging the MOHRE decision.

Article 54 provides that the judgment issued by the competent court in this context is a final judgment.

The purpose of this mechanism is clear: to avoid converting a dispute that has already passed through the administrative adjudication process into prolonged multi-level litigation.

The legislative approach therefore combines:

  • administrative adjudication
  • enforceability
  • a limited judicial challenge period
  • expedited judicial proceedings; and
  • finality of the court judgment

6. What Happens to Labour Disputes Exceeding AED 50,000?

Not every individual labour dispute falls within MOHRE’s adjudicatory jurisdiction.

Where the dispute does not fall within the circumstances specified in Article 54(2), and the Ministry is unable to achieve an amicable settlement within the applicable period, the dispute is referred to the competent court.

The referral is accompanied by a memorandum summarizing:

  • the nature of the dispute
  • the parties’ respective arguments; and

MOHRE’s recommendation.

Accordingly, the AED 50,000 threshold should not be understood as establishing a general rule that all labour disputes above AED 50,000 bypass MOHRE.

Rather, it determines one of the circumstances in which MOHRE itself may adjudicate the dispute.

7. MOHRE’s Role in Protecting Employees During the Dispute

Article 54 also provides an important protective mechanism for employees.

During the dispute-resolution process, MOHRE may require the employer to continue paying the employee’s wages for a period of up to two months, where the dispute has resulted in the suspension of wage payments, subject to the applicable implementing regulations.

This provision reflects the protective nature of UAE labour legislation and recognizes that prolonged non-payment of wages may create serious financial consequences for employees while a dispute remains unresolved.

8. Protection Against the Escalation of Individual Labour Disputes

Article 54 also empowers the Minister to impose additional administrative procedures or measures on an establishment where an individual labour dispute may develop into a collective labour dispute that could prejudice the public interest.

This demonstrates that the statutory framework is not concerned exclusively with determining monetary entitlement.

It also seeks to preserve:

  • workplace stability
  • continuity of employment relationships
  • labour-market stability; and
  • the public interest

9. Procedural Compliance Is Essential

A particularly important aspect of Article 54 is the requirement to comply with the statutory procedures and deadlines.

The legislation provides that a court will not accept an action concerning the disputes covered by Article 54 unless the procedures and time limits established by the provision have been followed.

For employers and employees, this makes procedural strategy as important as the substantive merits of the claim.

A party considering challenging a MOHRE decision should therefore examine, at minimum:

  • the date of notification of the decision
  • the nature and amount of the claim
  • the legal basis of the MOHRE decision
  • the evidence submitted during the MOHRE proceedings
  • whether the dispute falls within Article 54(2)
  • whether the statutory challenge period has been observed; and
  • whether enforcement has been suspended following the filing of the court action

10. Limitation Period for Labour Claims

Article 54 also establishes a limitation period for claims arising under the UAE Labour Law.

The current legislative framework provides that claims relating to rights arising under the Labour Law may not be heard after the statutory limitation period has expired.

Accordingly, employees and employers should not assume that employment-related claims can be brought indefinitely after the termination of the employment relationship.

Because limitation provisions have been subject to legislative amendments, the applicable version of Article 54 should always be verified based on the relevant date and the legislation in force at the time.

11. Practical Example: An Employee Claims AED 35,000

Consider a hypothetical case in which an employee claims AED 35,000 against an employer, consisting of:

  • AED 12,000 in unpaid wages
  • AED 8,000 in end-of-service benefits
  • AED 5,000 in unused annual leave

AED 7,000 in notice-period compensation; and

AED 3,000 in other contractual entitlements.

The total claim is AED 35,000.

Because the claim does not exceed AED 50,000, it falls within the monetary threshold established by Article 54(2), assuming the dispute otherwise falls within the scope of the provision.

MOHRE may therefore proceed beyond an ordinary attempt at amicable settlement and issue a decision determining the dispute.

If the Ministry orders the employer to pay the employee AED 35,000, that decision has the force of an enforceable instrument.

If either party disagrees with the decision, the party may challenge it before the competent court within the statutory 15-working-day period.

Once the court action is filed, enforcement of the MOHRE decision is suspended pending determination of the judicial proceedings.

12. Why the 2023 Amendment Is Significant

The amendment introduced by Federal Decree-Law No. (20) of 2023 represents an important development in UAE labour dispute resolution.

Previously, MOHRE’s role was primarily associated with facilitating amicable settlement and referring unresolved disputes to the competent judiciary.

The amended framework gives MOHRE a more substantial adjudicatory role in specified cases.

The practical consequences include:

Faster dispute resolution

Certain relatively low-value employment disputes can be determined administratively without beginning ordinary court proceedings from the outset.

Reduced judicial workload

The mechanism may reduce the number of relatively straightforward labour disputes reaching the courts.

Greater enforcement efficiency

A qualifying MOHRE decision is given the force of an enforceable instrument.

Continued access to judicial review

The parties retain a right to challenge the decision before the competent court within the statutory period.

Accelerated judicial proceedings

Where a challenge is brought, Article 54 establishes expedited procedural timelines.

MOHRE’s own data indicates that the 2023 amendment has been associated with a substantial increase in labour disputes resolved through the Ministry’s mechanisms and a decrease in disputes referred to the judiciary.

Conclusion

The amended Article 54 of the UAE Labour Law has created a distinctive mechanism for resolving individual labour disputes involving claims of AED 50,000 or less.

The significance of the mechanism lies not merely in the monetary threshold, but in the transformation of MOHRE’s role from primarily facilitating amicable settlement to, in specified circumstances, adjudicating the dispute and issuing a decision having the force of an enforceable instrument.

The system can therefore be understood as a three-stage framework:

  1. MOHRE receives and examines the labour complaint.
  2. MOHRE attempts to achieve an amicable settlement and, where Article 54(2) applies, may adjudicate the dispute and issue an enforceable decision.
  3. Either party may challenge the decision before the competent court within 15 working days, with the filing of the action suspending enforcement of the MOHRE decision.

For employers and employees, understanding this framework is essential. A dispute involving an amount of AED 50,000 or less is no longer necessarily a dispute that must proceed directly through conventional court litigation. Under the amended Article 54, MOHRE may have the statutory authority to determine the dispute itself.

Accordingly, anyone involved in a UAE employment dispute should carefully assess the value of the claim, the nature of the dispute, the applicable MOHRE procedure, the evidence supporting the claim or defence, and the strict statutory deadlines for challenging a Ministry decision.
