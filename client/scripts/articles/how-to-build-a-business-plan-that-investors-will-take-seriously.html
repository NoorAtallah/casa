A business plan should do more than describe your business.

For investors, it should answer a more important question:

Why should I invest in this business?

A strong business plan connects the business opportunity, market, strategy, financial performance and investment requirements into one credible story.

Investors do not expect every projection to be perfect. They do expect the assumptions, numbers and strategy to make sense.

What Makes a Business Plan Attractive to Investors?

A credible investor-focused business plan should clearly demonstrate:

  • A real and identifiable market opportunity
  • A compelling business model
  • A realistic growth strategy
  • A capable management team
  • Strong financial logic
  • Clear funding requirements
  • An understanding of key risks
  • A credible path to returns

The objective is not to make the business look perfect.

It is to make the investment opportunity understandable, credible and commercially compelling.

1. Start With the Investment Opportunity

Before going into operational details, explain why the business is worth investing in.

A strong opening should quickly communicate:

  • What the business does
  • What problem it solves
  • Who it serves
  • Why the market opportunity exists
  • What makes the business different
  • What the business is seeking from investors

An investor should be able to understand the opportunity without reading twenty pages.

2. Define the Problem and the Solution

Investors need to understand why customers need the business.

Clearly explain:

The problem: What gap, inefficiency or unmet need exists?

The solution: How does your product or service address it?

The value proposition: Why would customers choose your solution over alternatives?

Avoid relying on broad statements such as "there is strong demand."

Show the commercial reason behind the demand.

3. Demonstrate the Market Opportunity

A business plan should demonstrate that there is a sufficiently attractive market.

Consider addressing:

  • Market size
  • Target customers
  • Customer segments
  • Market growth
  • Industry trends
  • Competitive landscape
  • Barriers to entry

The key is to distinguish between a large market and a market the business can realistically capture.

Investors are interested in the second.

4. Explain How the Business Makes Money

Your business model should be easy to understand.

Explain:

  • Revenue streams
  • Pricing model
  • Customer acquisition
  • Sales channels
  • Recurring versus one-time revenue
  • Key operating costs
  • Profit drivers

An investor should understand how revenue becomes profit.

5. Show What Makes the Business Different

Competition exists in almost every market.

Instead of simply claiming to be "unique," explain what creates a genuine competitive advantage.

This could include:

  • Intellectual property
  • Technology
  • Brand
  • Distribution
  • Strategic partnerships
  • Customer relationships
  • Expertise
  • Cost advantages
  • Market positioning

The question investors are asking is:

What prevents another business from doing the same thing?

6. Build Financial Projections That Investors Can Trust

Financial projections are one of the most closely examined parts of an investor business plan.

Typically, investors will want to understand:

  • Revenue projections
  • Gross margin
  • Operating expenses
  • EBITDA or operating profit
  • Cash flow
  • Break-even point
  • Funding requirements
  • Capital expenditure

The numbers should be supported by reasonable assumptions.

For example, if revenue is expected to grow significantly, the plan should explain what drives that growth—more customers, higher prices, new locations, additional products or another identifiable factor.

7. Be Clear About How Much Funding You Need

Do not simply state:

"We are seeking AED 5 million."

Explain:

  • How much funding is required
  • What it will be used for
  • When it will be required
  • What milestones it will support
  • How the funding contributes to growth

Investors want to know where their money goes and what it is expected to achieve.

8. Address Risks Honestly

A credible business plan should acknowledge its key risks.

These may include:

  • Market competition
  • Regulatory changes
  • Customer concentration
  • Operational dependency
  • Funding requirements
  • Supply chain risks
  • Technology risks

More importantly, explain how management intends to manage those risks.

Acknowledging risks does not necessarily weaken an investment case.

It can demonstrate that management understands the business.

9. Present a Strong Management Team

Investors invest in businesses, but they also invest in people.

Show why the management team has the experience and capability to execute the plan.

Highlight relevant:

  • Industry experience
  • Leadership experience
  • Technical expertise
  • Previous achievements
  • Network and relationships
  • Track record

Keep the focus on why this team can deliver the strategy.

10. Explain the Path to Investor Returns

Depending on the investment, investors may want to understand how they can ultimately realise value.

This may involve:

  • Dividends
  • Business growth
  • Future investment rounds
  • Strategic acquisition
  • Sale of the company
  • Other potential exit opportunities

The business plan does not need to promise a specific outcome.

It should demonstrate that management has considered how investor value can be created.

What Investors Often Reject

A business plan can lose credibility when it contains:

  • Unrealistic financial projections
  • Unsupported market figures
  • Excessive focus on the founder
  • Vague competitive advantages
  • No clear funding requirement
  • Inconsistent financial assumptions
  • Too much operational detail
  • No explanation of key risks

More information does not necessarily make a business plan more convincing.

Clarity does.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a business plan include for investors?

An investor-focused business plan should generally cover the business opportunity, problem and solution, market, business model, competitive advantage, strategy, management team, financial projections, funding requirements and key risks.

How long should an investor business plan be?

There is no universal ideal length. It should be long enough to demonstrate the investment case but concise enough to maintain the investor's attention. The level of detail should reflect the complexity of the business and investment.

What financial information do investors look for?

Investors commonly review revenue, margins, operating costs, profitability, cash flow, break-even expectations, funding requirements and financial projections, together with the assumptions behind them.

How do I make my business plan more credible?

Use realistic assumptions, support important claims with evidence, ensure the financial model is consistent with the strategy, clearly explain risks and avoid unsupported projections.

What do investors want to know before investing?

Investors generally want to understand the opportunity, potential return, risks, competitive position, management capability and how their investment will be used to create value.
