The UAE and Europe have strong and growing commercial relationships, creating opportunities for businesses to trade, invest, establish partnerships and expand across both markets. The European Union describes the UAE as an important trade and investment partner, with EU–UAE trade and investment relations continuing to develop.

For businesses operating between the UAE and Europe, however, commercial opportunity needs to be matched with careful legal and regulatory planning.

A UAE company entering a European market—and a European company expanding into the UAE—may need to navigate different rules concerning corporate structures, contracts, licensing, taxation, employment, data protection, intellectual property and dispute resolution.

The legal requirements will also depend on the specific European country involved. Europe is not a single legal jurisdiction for all business matters, and requirements can differ between EU Member States and other European countries.

1. Choose the Right Business Structure

One of the first decisions is how the business will establish its presence in the other market.

Depending on the circumstances, this could involve:

  • A subsidiary
  • A branch
  • A joint venture
  • A local partnership
  • A distribution arrangement
  • A commercial agency
  • A contractual relationship without establishing a local entity

The appropriate structure depends on the nature of the business, ownership, activities, regulatory requirements and long-term objectives.

For UAE businesses entering Europe, and European businesses entering the UAE, the structure should be assessed before significant commercial commitments are made.

2. Understand Local Licensing Requirements

A business cannot assume that a licence obtained in one jurisdiction automatically permits it to conduct the same activities in another.

Before commencing operations, businesses should determine:

  • Whether local registration is required
  • Whether the activity is regulated
  • Whether sector-specific approval is necessary
  • Whether a local establishment is required
  • Whether professional qualifications need recognition
  • Whether import or export requirements apply

The UAE itself provides different routes for establishing and operating businesses, including mainland structures and free-zone options.

The same principle applies when entering European markets: the requirements should be assessed in the specific country and sector concerned.

3. Structure Cross-Border Contracts Carefully

International commercial relationships should be supported by contracts that clearly allocate responsibilities and risks.

Key provisions may include:

  • Scope of services
  • Payment terms
  • Delivery obligations
  • Confidentiality
  • Intellectual property
  • Representations and warranties
  • Liability
  • Indemnities
  • Termination
  • Governing law
  • Dispute resolution

A contract drafted for a purely domestic transaction may not adequately address the additional risks of a UAE–Europe relationship.

4. Decide Which Law Will Govern the Contract

A UAE company contracting with a European business should not simply assume that UAE law—or the counterparty's local law—will automatically apply.

The parties should consider:

Which law should govern the agreement?

This decision can affect how contractual obligations, liability, termination rights and other provisions are interpreted.

The governing law should therefore be considered as part of the commercial negotiation, rather than treated as standard wording at the end of the contract.

5. Plan for Dispute Resolution

Cross-border disputes can become more complicated because the parties, assets and evidence may be located in different jurisdictions.

Contracts should clearly address:

  • Court jurisdiction or arbitration
  • Arbitration seat, where applicable
  • Applicable procedural rules
  • Enforcement considerations
  • Mediation or other pre-dispute procedures

A well-designed dispute resolution clause can provide significantly greater certainty if a commercial relationship breaks down.

6. Pay Particular Attention to Data Protection

Data protection is especially important when businesses transfer personal information between the UAE and Europe.

The EU GDPR can apply to organisations outside the EU where they offer goods or services to individuals in the EU or monitor their behaviour.

EU rules also provide specific mechanisms for transferring personal data outside the European Economic Area, including appropriate safeguards such as contractual mechanisms in relevant circumstances.

For a UAE business working with European customers, employees or business partners, it is therefore important to determine:

  • What personal data is being processed?
  • Where is it stored?
  • Who has access?
  • Where is it transferred?
  • Which data protection laws apply?
  • What contractual safeguards are required?

Data protection should be considered at the beginning of the relationship—not after the data starts moving across borders.

7. Protect Intellectual Property

International expansion can expose valuable intellectual property to new markets and commercial partners.

Businesses should consider protection for:

  • Trademarks
  • Copyright
  • Patents
  • Trade secrets
  • Software
  • Brand assets
  • Proprietary business information

Intellectual property rights are generally territorial, so protection obtained in one jurisdiction may not automatically provide equivalent protection elsewhere.

Businesses expanding between the UAE and Europe should therefore identify where their key intellectual property needs protection.

8. Consider Tax and Permanent Establishment Issues

Cross-border business can create tax considerations that should be assessed alongside the legal structure.

These may include:

  • Corporate tax
  • VAT
  • Withholding tax
  • Transfer pricing
  • Permanent establishment
  • Tax residency
  • Double-taxation considerations

The UAE also imposes corporate tax on the net income or profit of businesses within its applicable framework, alongside VAT and other taxes.

The tax position can become more complex where a company has employees, offices, agents or significant business activities in another jurisdiction.

Legal and tax advice should therefore be coordinated rather than considered separately.

9. Review Employment Arrangements

Hiring employees in another jurisdiction can create obligations that differ substantially from those in the company's home country.

Businesses should consider:

  • Employment contracts
  • Employee rights
  • Working conditions
  • Benefits
  • Termination requirements
  • Immigration
  • Social security
  • Local employment regulations

A UAE business employing people in Europe, for example, should not assume that its UAE employment documentation will automatically be appropriate for the European jurisdiction involved.

10. Conduct Due Diligence on Business Partners

Before entering a significant relationship with a European or UAE business, appropriate due diligence can help identify potential risks.

Depending on the transaction, this may include reviewing:

  • Corporate registration
  • Ownership
  • Directors
  • Financial position
  • Litigation
  • Regulatory compliance
  • Licences
  • Reputation
  • Existing contractual obligations

This becomes particularly important for joint ventures, acquisitions, strategic partnerships and major distribution arrangements.

11. Consider Competition and Investment Regulations

Larger transactions may involve additional regulatory considerations.

These can include:

  • Competition law
  • Merger control
  • Foreign investment screening
  • Sector-specific restrictions
  • State-related requirements

The EU's current trade discussions with the UAE include areas such as services and investment, digital trade, intellectual property, competition, merger control and dispute settlement—illustrating the breadth of regulatory issues relevant to UAE–EU commercial relations.

This is another reason why a cross-border transaction should be assessed based on its specific structure, sector and jurisdictions involved.

12. Consider Import, Export and Customs Requirements

Businesses trading goods between the UAE and Europe may need to address:

  • Customs requirements
  • Rules of origin
  • Product standards
  • Import licences
  • Export controls
  • Labelling requirements
  • Product-specific regulations
  • Customs duties

These requirements can vary significantly depending on the product and destination.

Businesses should establish the applicable requirements before entering into long-term supply commitments.

13. Do Not Assume That “Europe” Means One Set of Rules

This is an important point for businesses.

The European Union has common legal frameworks in many areas, but individual Member States can have their own national rules and procedures.

And Europe extends beyond the EU.

Therefore, a UAE business should avoid treating:

“Europe” as one legal market.

Instead, assess the specific country, industry, transaction and activity involved.

The Key Principle

Doing business between the UAE and Europe can offer significant opportunities, but commercial opportunity should not be separated from legal preparation.

Before signing an agreement, establishing an entity, appointing a partner or entering a new market, businesses should understand:

Who are we contracting with? Where will we operate? Which laws apply? What risks are we accepting? How will disputes be resolved? How will we protect our assets and information?

The earlier these questions are addressed, the easier it becomes to structure the relationship appropriately.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main legal considerations when doing business between the UAE and Europe?

The main considerations include business structure, licensing, contracts, governing law, dispute resolution, data protection, intellectual property, tax, employment, due diligence and regulatory compliance.

Does a UAE company need a European company to do business in Europe?

Not necessarily. The appropriate structure depends on the company's activities, the specific European jurisdiction, regulatory requirements and commercial objectives.

Does GDPR apply to UAE companies?

It can. GDPR may apply to organisations outside the EU where they offer goods or services to individuals in the EU or monitor their behaviour.

Which law should govern a UAE–Europe commercial contract?

There is no universal answer. The appropriate governing law depends on the transaction, parties, jurisdictions, commercial objectives and applicable mandatory laws.

Should UAE companies conduct legal due diligence before entering Europe?

Yes. Appropriate due diligence can help identify corporate, contractual, regulatory, financial and reputational risks before entering a significant business relationship.

What should a UAE business consider before expanding into Europe?

It should assess market entry structure, licensing, contracts, taxation, data protection, employment, intellectual property, regulatory requirements and the specific laws of the target country.

Are UAE–Europe business transactions subject to EU law?

Potentially. The applicable legal framework depends on the nature of the activity, the parties, the jurisdiction and whether the transaction falls within relevant EU or national rules.
